Overview and definitions

Orienteering is a sport where competitors use a map and compass to navigate from point to point as fast as possible. Controls are physical markers on the course, competitors must navigate their way between these to complete their course. The terrain in which courses are set can vary significantly, traditionally events are usually set in forests or bushland, however the sport has expanded to include events set in schools and universities, and on large segments of a city or town.

The most common type of orienteering is done on foot, but there are events that use mountain bikes or skis to move, and other events that use radio direction finding transmitters rather than maps.

Types of events

The types of events for orienteering are typically classified into three categories. Long distance events are nearly exclusively in forests or bushland and can be up to 15 km as the crow flies.

Middle distance is primarily comprised of Park & Street events. These events are run on very simplified maps in city suburbs and typically range from 3–10 km in length.

Sprint events are short events typically 2–4 km in length. These events are run mostly on school or university campuses and require far more technical high-speed navigation.
